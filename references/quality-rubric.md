# Quality rubric

Audit the episode in two passes. Do not let strong prose compensate for weak evidence.

## Source pass

### Factual grounding

Pass when every material factual claim is directly supported or clearly labeled as synthesis or interpretation.

Fail for:

- claims that exceed the source
- missing limitations that change the meaning
- correlation presented as causation
- a proposal presented as a demonstrated result
- invented quantities, dates, examples, quotations, or consensus
- host opinions presented as the authors' conclusions

### Evidence coverage

Pass when the episode selects enough evidence to answer its central question without pretending to summarize every section.

Fail when important counterevidence or uncertainty is excluded solely because it weakens the narrative.

## Listening pass

Score each dimension from 1 to 5 and give concrete evidence for the score.

### Structure

- The opening establishes a clear promise.
- One central question drives the episode.
- Each movement creates the need for the next.
- The ending delivers or deliberately reframes the promise.

### Conversational naturalness

- The hosts respond to what was just said.
- Turn lengths and sentence shapes vary.
- Questions reflect plausible curiosity or confusion.
- Speaker labels could not be swapped without changing the episode.

### Learning value

- Difficult concepts are unpacked rather than merely renamed.
- At least one example tests the explanation.
- Reasoning steps are audible.
- Qualifications remain understandable.

### Accessibility

- Jargon is defined when first needed.
- The listener is not expected to remember long lists.
- Numbers and abbreviations are phrased for speech.
- Dense passages use signposts, examples, or restatement.

### Host dynamics

- Both hosts remain capable adults.
- Expertise rotates when appropriate.
- Agreement and disagreement arise from the content.
- Humor or emotion does not feel manufactured.
- Neither host always wins.

### Pacing

- The script gives complex ideas enough space.
- Short turns clarify rather than fragment the explanation.
- Repetition serves comprehension rather than padding.
- The estimated runtime matches the requested scope.

### Ending payoff

- The final idea is specific to this episode.
- The listener receives a useful distinction, question, or decision rule.
- The script stops without an unnecessary second ending.

## Automatic revision triggers

Revise before delivery if any of these appear:

- an unsupported material claim
- an abrupt change of subject
- several consecutive alternating mini-lectures
- repeated “Exactly,” “Absolutely,” or equivalent empty agreement
- a question whose only purpose is to cue exposition
- a perfect paraphrase that adds no learning value
- a predictable assertion/counterassertion chain
- invented host biography or experience
- forced reconciliation
- a generic conclusion that could fit any episode

## Report format

Include:

- pass or revise
- unsupported or uncertain claims
- scores with one-sentence evidence
- strongest moment
- weakest moment
- exact high-priority revisions
- word count and explicitly assumed speaking pace
- estimated runtime, clearly labeled as an estimate
